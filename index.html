Part C — Launch the Project: “Story in a Box” (20 min) Set up the shared universe. Put students into groups of 3–4. Each group gets a “box” (or a set of 8–10 prompt slips you pre-make—paper strips). Everyone draws: A setting A character quirk An object with meaning A conflict They must agree on one sentence that becomes their “universe hook”: “In [setting], a [character/quirk] must face [conflict] after [object appears].” Wrap: Collect the group hooks to post/display (so students feel like they “started something” on Day 1). Day 2: Drafting Day (Fun Constraints) + Peer Warm Feedback (45–90 min) Goal Turn creativity into an actual draft using playful structure + confidence. Part A — Writing Warm-Up: “30-Second Cinematic” (8–10 min) Students pick one group-universe hook from the board/poster. They write 3 quick bullet images: What does it look like? What does it sound like? What does the main character want right now? Part B — Mini Lesson: “Show, Don’t Tell… but Make It Weird” (10 min) Teach one small craft target: Choose one: vivid sensory details purposeful dialogue a character action that reveals emotion Give a 2-sentence model and ask students to annotate it (“What makes this show, not tell?”) Part C — Individual Writing Task: “Scene with a Twist” (25–45 min) Each student writes a 1–2 page scene connected to their chosen group universe. Constraint to make it fun (and easier to start): Must include: 1 setting detail (specific and concrete) 1 object (from the group draws/box) 1 dialogue exchange (at least 4 lines total) 1 sentence that starts with: “Of course,” or “I didn’t mean to…” (fun rule!) A twist at the end (something is not what it seemed) Part D — Low-Stakes Peer Feedback: “Compliment + Question” (10–15 min) Pair students and use a simple form: Glow (compliment): “I liked how you…” Grow (question): “I’m curious about…” Collect the questions; you can respond tomorrow as a class. Day 3: Revise + Share (Conference + Optional Performance) (45–90 min) Goal Improve writing without making it feel like “serious test prep.” Part A — Revision Sprint: “One Target Only” (10 min) Give students a checklist, but they choose only one focus: Pick one: stronger ending (twist or emotional shift) clearer character motivation more vivid sensory detail revise dialogue for realism/purpose Students do a “revision sprint” of 10 minutes. Part B — Teacher Micro-Conferences (15–25 min) While students revise, you conference with 6–8 students total: ask: “What are you trying to make the reader feel?” identify one actionable next step (If you can’t conference everyone, do quick spot-checks and short notes.) Part C — Share Day: “Gallery Walk: Sticky Notes” OR “Read-Aloud Choice” (15–25 min) Two options depending on your class vibe: Option 1: Gallery Walk (low pressure) Post excerpts (or students turn in) and students walk with a sticky note prompt: “This line made me visualize…” “I felt… when…” “I wondered…” Option 2: Optional Performance Students may read a 30–45 second excerpt aloud if they want. Not required. Part D — Closure Reflection (5–8 min) Students answer one prompt: “Today I improved my writing by…” “My next writing goal is…” Materials to Prep (quick list) Slips for the “Story in a Box”: settings (school, coastal town, future city, rural farm, etc.) quirk traits (overly literal, collects receipts, can’t lie, hears music in everything) objects (compass that points to memories, broken watch that runs backward, key without a door) conflicts (a rumor spreads, someone vanishes, a promise is tested, the object “chooses” a person) Procedure prompts for Day 1 A simple peer feedback sheet (Glow/Grow) Why this works for a “catch-up” class Students collaborate immediately but still write independently. The shared universe reduces “blank page stress.” Revision + feedback helps you quickly gauge skill levels and personality.
